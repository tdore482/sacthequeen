How to read an opening

Every good opening does three jobs at once. If you remember nothing else, remember these.

01

Claim the center

Pawns on e4 and d4, or a pawn on c4 backed by Nf3, strike at the middle. From there your pieces reach either wing and change direction quickly.

02

Develop with a purpose

Bring out knights and bishops to squares where they do something. A move that both develops and threatens is usually worth more than a cautious retreat.

03

Get the king safe

Castling tucks the king away and connects the rooks. Until that happens, the center is a dangerous place for your king to linger.

Ideas beat memorization

The Idea line explains the tension in plain language. The Plans line gives the typical follow-up for both colors. Neither is a promise: your opponent gets a vote, and a changed pawn structure can change the whole plan.

A pawn break such as ...c5 or d4 is a move that challenges the center. When you see one, ask what happens if it is captured, ignored, or pushed past. That question is most of opening strategy.

New here? Start with the Italian Game, Queen's Gambit Declined, or London System. They teach the same lessons with fewer forcing lines.
